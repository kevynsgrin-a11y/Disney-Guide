May at Universal Orlando and Universal Hollywood. Your month in about three minutes, from Hollywood Ride Guide.

Our grade for May is B plus. The best month of the first half of the year that is not February. Long hours, warm weather, crowds that have not yet turned over to summer, and prices that step up only at the very end.

Everything in this brief was checked in July 2026. Nothing here is a forecast for a specific date. These are multi-year patterns we plan against, not a promise.

Crowds. May runs moderate. May is the calm between spring break and summer, and it lasts about three weeks. US schools are in session for most of the month, spring break travel has finished, and the summer wave has not started, so Universal Orlando runs moderate on weekdays and busy on weekends without ever reaching the flat plateau June brings.

The stretch to avoid: the Memorial Day long weekend at both resorts, the first summer-level crowd of the year.

If you have flexibility: the first two weeks, particularly Tuesday through Thursday in Orlando.

Weather. Climate normals over a thirty-year reference period, not a forecast.

Universal Orlando: highs around 89 degrees, lows around 67, about 8 days with measurable rain. Hot but not yet oppressive: high 80s afternoons with humidity that is climbing through the month, and eight rain days rather than the seventeen July brings. The afternoon thunderstorm pattern usually establishes itself in the last week or two, which is the point at which an Orlando day starts needing a midday plan.

Universal Hollywood: highs around 80 degrees, lows around 57, about 2 days with measurable rain. Effectively dry, with afternoons around 80 and evenings in the high 50s. The coastal marine layer often keeps mornings grey and cool into midday this month, which is a local pattern visitors mistake for bad weather and which usually burns off by lunchtime.

Cost. May sits in the moderate band. May prices in the middle of the Universal Orlando year and does not stay there. The first three weeks carry mid-tier ticket dates, hotel rates below the summer level, and Express Pass pricing that responds mostly to the day of the week rather than to the season.

One thing worth knowing. The three-week window is the point of this month. The first three weeks of May are the last stretch of the year when Universal Orlando is warm, open late, and not crowded. Ride waits sit closer to February's than to July's, operating hours are within an hour or so of their summer maximum, and the water attractions at Islands of Adventure are comfortable rather than a gamble.

Who May suits. Longer park days, since operating hours reach close to their summer length.

Who it does not. The Memorial Day weekend, which is the first genuine peak of the summer season.
