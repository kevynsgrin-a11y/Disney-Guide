January at Universal Orlando and Universal Hollywood. Your month in about three minutes, from Hollywood Ride Guide.

Our grade for January is A minus. From the second week onward this is the best-value month at both resorts. You trade long days and a complete ride list for low crowds and low prices, which is a trade most visitors should take.

Everything in this brief was checked in July 2026. Nothing here is a forecast for a specific date. These are multi-year patterns we plan against, not a promise.

Crowds. January runs low. January splits hard in two, and the boundary is the end of the winter school holiday rather than the calendar. The first week is still peak: Orlando runs on international and domestic holiday travel that does not unwind until schools go back, and Universal Hollywood carries the tail of its holiday overlay with Southern California families who are off until the second week.

The stretch to avoid: the first week, which is holiday season in everything but name at both resorts.

If you have flexibility: the second and third full weeks, the quietest stretch of the Orlando year.

Weather. Climate normals over a thirty-year reference period, not a forecast.

Universal Orlando: highs around 71 degrees, lows around 50, about 7 days with measurable rain. The most comfortable park weather of the Universal Orlando year and the month most likely to be genuinely cold: overnight lows near 50 mean a morning in the low 50s is normal, and a cold front can drop that into the 30s for a couple of days. Pack layers rather than a coat, and remember that the water rides at Islands of Adventure are miserable below about 65 degrees and often close outright.

Universal Hollywood: highs around 68 degrees, lows around 45, about 6 days with measurable rain. January is the wettest month of the Los Angeles year, and while six rain days out of thirty-one still means most days are dry, the Studio Tour is an open tram and the Lower Lot is reached by open escalators. Evenings drop into the 40s, which is colder than most visitors packing for California expect.

Cost. January sits in the low band. January past the first week is the cheapest window Universal sells. Orlando hotel rates sit at their annual floor, date-based ticket pricing sits in its bottom tier on most weekdays, and Express Pass, which is priced by date and moves more sharply than anything else Universal sells, reaches its lowest published levels of the year on quiet midweek dates.

One thing worth knowing. The holiday overlays come down early, and nobody warns you. Both resorts strip their holiday layer within the first fortnight, and the exact date is not published far ahead. Holidays at Universal Orlando and Grinchmas at Universal Hollywood have both historically run into the first week of January and then stopped, which means a trip booked for the third week gets January prices and no seasonal content at all.

Who January suits. Anyone whose priority is walking onto rides rather than seeing everything the resort has.

Who it does not. The first week of the month, which is still holiday-season pricing and holiday-season crowds.
