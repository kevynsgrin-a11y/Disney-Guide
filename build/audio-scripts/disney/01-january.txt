January at Walt Disney World and Disneyland Resort. Your month in about three minutes, from Ride Ready Guide.

Our grade for January is A minus. The quietest, cheapest four weeks at both resorts once New Year's traffic clears. The catch is short park hours and the heaviest refurbishment schedule of the year.

Everything in this brief was checked in July 2026. Nothing here is a forecast for a specific date. These are multi-year patterns we plan against, not a promise.

Crowds. January runs low. Attendance does not taper after New Year's, it falls off a shelf. Once the holiday return-travel wave clears in the first several days, every driver of theme park demand switches off simultaneously: no US school district is on break, the Florida drive-in market has spent its money, and international leisure travel is at its annual trough.

The stretch to avoid: the first several days, until holiday return travel finishes unwinding.

If you have flexibility: the second and third full weeks at Walt Disney World, the lowest waits of the year.

Weather. Climate normals over a thirty-year reference period, not a forecast.

Walt Disney World: highs around 72 degrees, lows around 51, about 7 days with measurable rain. January is the mildest month of the year here and one of the two driest, with low humidity and comfortable afternoons. The variance is what catches people out: a cold front can hold highs in the 50s and lows in the 30s for two or three days at a stretch, and Orlando's parks have very little indoor waiting space when it does.

Disneyland Resort: highs around 69 degrees, lows around 46, about 6 days with measurable rain. January is one of Southern California's three wettest months, and the rain arrives as multi-day frontal systems rather than as the twenty-minute showers Florida gets. Sunny January days in Anaheim are genuinely pleasant and the evenings are cold enough that a fleece is not optional for a nighttime show.

Cost. January sits in the low band. Every lever moves the same direction at once, which is rare. Walt Disney World's date-based ticket tiers sit at or near their annual floor for most of the month; resort hotels drop into their lowest published rate season once the holiday period ends; and airfare into Orlando collapses as soon as holiday return travel finishes, because the leisure market is spent and the convention calendar has not restarted.

One thing worth knowing. Short lines, short days. The trap in January is treating a low crowd level as a long day. Operating hours are cut back sharply once the holiday period ends, and a Magic Kingdom that closed at midnight in late December may close at 8 PM three weeks later, with EPCOT and Animal Kingdom closing earlier still.

Who January suits. Adults and couples who want short lines more than they want a full ride roster.

Who it does not. The first week of the month, which is still holiday-week attendance at holiday-week prices.
