September at Walt Disney World and Disneyland Resort. Your month in about three minutes, from Ride Ready Guide.

Our grade for September is A. The lowest waits and the lowest prices of the year at Walt Disney World, bought with heat and storm risk. Disneyland is a different answer, Halloween keeps its weekends genuinely busy.

Everything in this brief was checked in July 2026. Nothing here is a forecast for a specific date. These are multi-year patterns we plan against, not a promise.

Crowds. September runs low. Every driver of Walt Disney World attendance switches off at once in September and nothing replaces them. US districts are back in session nationwide, the Florida drive-in market has exhausted its summer budget, the international summer season has ended, and hurricane risk deters everybody who is not on a Disney-specific mission.

The stretch to avoid: labor Day weekend at both resorts.

If you have flexibility: the middle three weeks at Walt Disney World, the lowest waits of the year.

Weather. Climate normals over a thirty-year reference period, not a forecast.

Walt Disney World: highs around 90 degrees, lows around 73, about 14 days with measurable rain. September is still summer in every practical sense, with the afternoon thunderstorm pattern intact through most of the month and fourteen days of measurable rain. It is also the statistical peak of the Atlantic hurricane season, which is the entire reason this month is as cheap and as quiet as it is.

Disneyland Resort: highs around 84 degrees, lows around 63, about 1 days with measurable rain. September is frequently hotter in practice than August, because the Santa Ana wind pattern begins and can push inland Orange County past 95 for several consecutive days. When that happens it is dry heat with cool evenings, which is a very different experience from Florida at the same temperature.

Cost. September sits in the low band. Walt Disney World resort hotels reach their annual rate floor here, and September is the month room-only discounts historically run deepest and cover the widest set of resort categories. Date-based ticket tiers sit in their lowest bands outside January.

One thing worth knowing. Buy the insurance, and buy it before a storm is named. September is the peak of the Atlantic season and the risk is real rather than notional. Central Florida storms rarely cause the parks to close for more than a day or two, but they routinely cause flight cancellations, and a two-day closure inside a five-day trip destroys the trip's value.

Who September suits. Adults, couples, and anyone without a school-calendar constraint.

Who it does not. Anyone booking non-refundable travel.
