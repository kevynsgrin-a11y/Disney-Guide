November at Walt Disney World and Disneyland Resort. Your month in about three minutes, from Ride Ready Guide.

Our grade for November is B plus. You can buy December's product on November's prices for about two weeks, then Thanksgiving arrives. One local school break in early November is the trap most planners miss.

Everything in this brief was checked in July 2026. Nothing here is a forecast for a specific date. These are multi-year patterns we plan against, not a promise.

Crowds. November runs high. November has the sharpest internal contrast of any month on the calendar. The first two weeks are among the year's better windows, good weather, holiday decorations already installed, and no national break, with one local exception that catches people out repeatedly.

The stretch to avoid: thanksgiving week, from the weekend before through the weekend after, at both resorts.

If you have flexibility: the stretch between Jersey Week and the Thanksgiving run-up, the best value window of the year's back half.

Weather. Climate normals over a thirty-year reference period, not a forecast.

Walt Disney World: highs around 79 degrees, lows around 60, about 6 days with measurable rain. The dry season is fully established and Orlando is comfortable from open to close for the first time since March. Evenings drop into the 50s often enough that a jacket is not optional for a nighttime show, and a late-month cold front can take the overnight low into the 40s.

Disneyland Resort: highs around 73 degrees, lows around 50, about 3 days with measurable rain. Mild, mostly dry, and dark early, Anaheim sunsets fall before 5 PM for most of the month, which is excellent for the holiday lighting package and hard on anyone whose plan relied on daylight after a midday break.

Cost. November sits in the moderate band. The lever worth understanding this month is timing rather than product. Walt Disney World holds fall shoulder hotel rates until the Thanksgiving season begins, and the date-based ticket tiers in the first half of November are among the lowest outside January and September, while the Christmas decorations, the castle lighting package, the seasonal ride overlays, the holiday party nights, and the Festival of the Holidays are all already running.

One thing worth knowing. Jersey Week is the trap, and it is not on any calendar you already have. The New Jersey Education Association convention closes most New Jersey public schools for a couple of days in early November, and the resulting travel wave lands squarely on Walt Disney World. It reliably produces a busy week in what people have been told is a quiet month, and because it is a single-state break it appears in no national school calendar and no generic seasonal guide.

Who November suits. Holiday trips that want the decorations without December's crowds or rates.

Who it does not. Thanksgiving week, which runs peak from the weekend before through the weekend after.
