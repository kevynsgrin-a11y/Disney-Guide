Doing the Disney parks at a holiday

One page per holiday, covering both resorts: what actually runs, what it costs, and whether the crowds are worth it.

Holiday

Disney at Christmas: your actual options at both resorts, and what they cost

The holiday season at both resorts runs from early November to early January and the best two weeks of it are the first two, when everything is installed, nothing is sold out, and prices sit at their annual floor rather than their annual peak.

Holiday

Disney at Halloween: every option at both resorts, and what each one costs

Both US resorts run one paid Halloween party and one free daytime layer, and the value sits in opposite places on each coast — in Florida the party is the thing worth buying, in California the free overlay is the thing worth planning around.

Holiday

Disney on New Year's Eve: what is running, what it costs, and whether to go at all

Neither resort sells a New Year's Eve party, both hit their annual capacity ceiling on the night itself, and the honest verdict is that this is the worst-value date on the Disney calendar unless the date itself is the reason you are going.

Holiday

Disney at spring break and Easter: which weeks are survivable and what they cost

Spring break at Disney is a six-week rolling peak rather than a single week, and the difference between the best and worst week inside it is larger than the difference between spring and any other season — which makes date selection the only decision on this page that matters.

Holiday

Disney in summer: the weeks worth taking, the weeks to refuse, and what it costs

Summer at Disney splits into three distinct seasons — a brutal late-June-to-mid-July peak, a genuine trough in the last two weeks of August, and an autumn season that starts in mid-August — and the last two weeks of August are the best value of the entire year at Walt Disney World.
