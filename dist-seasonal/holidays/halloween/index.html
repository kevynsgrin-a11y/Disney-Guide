Disney at Halloween: every option at both resorts, and what each one costs

Both US resorts run one paid Halloween party and one free daytime layer, and the value sits in opposite places on each coast — in Florida the party is the thing worth buying, in California the free overlay is the thing worth planning around.

Your options, by resort

Both coasts do this differently, and the right answer depends on which one you are going to.

Walt Disney World

Halloween at Walt Disney World is a hard-ticket product. The daytime parks are decorated but nothing is rethemed, so the party is the season. Not-So-Scary runs at Magic Kingdom on roughly 30 to 40 nights from mid-August to Halloween and ran $119 to $219 per night in 2025. EPCOT's Food & Wine Festival runs concurrently at no extra charge beyond what you eat. Disney After Hours is the cheaper way to buy an empty park if the seasonal content is not what you are after.
  • Mickey's Not-So-Scary Halloween Party — Worth buying once, on an early-season weeknight, and only if you accept that you are paying for a parade, a fireworks show and character lines that exist on no other date — not for a night of short ride waits.
  • EPCOT International Food & Wine Festival — The best festival Disney runs and the most expensive way to eat at a theme park — go on a weekday, budget around $60 a head, and treat the booths as dinner rather than as snacks you graze between rides.
  • Disney After Hours — The best per-ride value Walt Disney World sells, and only if you ride hard for the full three hours — buy it at Hollywood Studios or Magic Kingdom, and do not buy it at Animal Kingdom.

Disneyland Resort

Halloween Time runs across Disneyland Park and Disney California Adventure from late August to the end of October and is included with any ticket, ride overlays and all. Oogie Boogie Bash is a separate hard-ticket night at Disney California Adventure only — not at Disneyland Park, which is the single most common booking error in this season. It ran $154 to $224 per night in 2025 on roughly 25 to 30 nights, and it sells out earlier than any other event at either resort.
  • Oogie Boogie Bash - A Disney Halloween Party — The best hard-ticket night either Disney resort runs, and the hardest one to buy: Oogie Boogie Bash sells out months before the season starts, which makes the ticket-buying strategy more important than anything that happens at the party itself.
  • Halloween Time at the Disneyland Resort — Included with any regular ticket, which is the whole argument for it, and the two attractions it repaints are better than the versions they replace. What you give up is those versions: Haunted Mansion Holiday is what you get instead of the Haunted Mansion, not in addition to it, and it holds the longest wait in its park all season.

The short answer

Buy one night of Mickey's Not-So-Scary Halloween Party in Florida, and skip Oogie Boogie Bash in California unless costumes are the reason for the trip. Not-So-Scary ran $119 to $219 per night across the 2025 season and buys you five hours of a thinly populated Magic Kingdom plus a parade and a fireworks show that exist on no other night. Oogie Boogie Bash ran $154 to $224 per night for the same cycle, sells out months earlier, and hands you a smaller park with a shorter ride list open.

The free layer inverts that. Halloween Time at the Disneyland Resort changes more of the parks for no extra money than anything Walt Disney World does in daylight: two ride overlays and a full seasonal cavalcade are included with any regular ticket. Walt Disney World has no Halloween ride overlay at all. If your budget is fixed and you are choosing a coast, Anaheim gives you more Halloween per dollar of ticket and Florida gives you more Halloween per dollar of event ticket.

One decision rule that covers most people: a first-time Florida family should buy exactly one party night and treat it as a full park day, entering at the early admission time. A Southern California local should ignore the paid party entirely and go on a September weekday, when the overlays are up and the crowds are not.

What the paid nights cost

Three products compete for the same money in the autumn, and their 2025-season bands were close enough that the choice is about content rather than price. Not-So-Scary ran $119 to $219 per night, Oogie Boogie Bash ran $154 to $224 per night, and Disney After Hours at Walt Disney World ran $139 to $209 per night. All three are before tax, and none of them requires a daytime park ticket on top.

That last point is the largest saving available in the whole season and most first-timers miss it. A party ticket admits you to the park several hours before the party officially starts, so a party night can be your park day. Buying a full-price day ticket and a party ticket for the same date wastes $119 to $209 in Florida for a few extra morning hours you will spend in the heat.

The pricing shape is identical at both resorts: the cheapest nights are early in the season and midweek, the most expensive are the final weekends before Halloween itself. The gap between the cheapest and dearest night of the same season runs to roughly $80 to $100 before tax, which is more than most people will save by any other tactic on this page.

If short lines rather than seasonal content are what you are buying, Disney After Hours at $139 to $209 is usually the better purchase. It is the same empty-park product without the parade, the costumes, or the sell-out risk, and it runs in months when hotel rates are lower.

What you get without buying anything extra

The Disneyland Resort's free Halloween layer is the strongest seasonal value on either coast. Halloween Time runs across both Anaheim parks from late August to the end of October, and it includes ride overlays that genuinely change the attraction rather than adding a pumpkin to the queue. Nothing about it costs more than the ticket you were buying anyway.

Walt Disney World's daytime Halloween presence is decoration, a handful of seasonal menu items, and merchandise. Magic Kingdom's autumn look is real and photographs well, but there is no overlay, no seasonal parade outside the party, and no reason to shift your dates for it. In Florida the season is behind a paywall in a way it is not in California.

The EPCOT International Food & Wine Festival runs straight through the Halloween window and is included with a regular EPCOT ticket. For a Florida party family it is the answer to the question of what to do on the two days either side of the party night, and it costs nothing to walk through — festival food items ran $5 to $13 each as of July 2026, which is where the money actually goes.

When the season actually starts, and when to go

Walt Disney World opens its Halloween season around the middle of August and the Disneyland Resort follows about ten days later, which means a mid-August trip catches Florida decorated and California not. Both seasons end on Halloween night — there is no extended-into-November Halloween at either resort, and the holiday overlays begin within days of the pumpkins coming down.

The best-value window is the last two weeks of August and the first three weeks of September. Party tickets sit at the bottom of their bands, hotel rates are at their annual low in Florida, school is back in most of the country, and the seasonal layer is fully installed at both resorts. The trade is heat in Orlando and the possibility of a hurricane day.

The worst-value window is the final ten days of October. Party nights price at the top of the band, they sell out first, and the daytime parks are busy with people who came for the same reason you did. Halloween itself is the single most expensive and most crowded night of the season at both resorts.

Sell-out risk, and how far ahead to book

Oogie Boogie Bash is the one event on this page that reliably sells out, and it sells out fast enough that it should drive your dates rather than fit around them. Tickets go on sale in the late spring, annual passholders get a presale window, and the late-October Friday and Saturday nights have historically gone within hours of general sale. If Oogie Boogie Bash is the point of the trip, book the event first and the flights second.

Not-So-Scary sells out selectively. Early-season Sunday and Tuesday nights are usually available into the season, and the last three Fridays before Halloween are the ones that go. Because Magic Kingdom runs so many more party nights than Disney California Adventure does, missing out entirely is unlikely; missing out on a specific date is common.

Neither resort has confirmed 2026 dates to us as of July 2026. Everything on this page is stated as a window or as a prior-cycle figure for that reason. Check the operator's own calendar before you commit money to a specific night.

Which resort to pick for Halloween

Pick the Disneyland Resort if you want Halloween to be the texture of your whole trip, and pick Walt Disney World if you want one big Halloween night and a large trip around it. That is the honest split, and price is not what decides it.

The Anaheim case: two parks a five-minute walk apart, ride overlays included with your ticket, a season that touches almost every corner of both parks, and a paid party you can skip without feeling short-changed. The Florida case: a party that runs on far more nights, a parade and fireworks that are worth the ticket on their own, a concurrent food festival, and enough park capacity that a party night genuinely feels empty.

Families with young children should note one thing that price comparisons hide. Oogie Boogie Bash's character content skews toward villains and its treat trail is the main draw for small children; Not-So-Scary's parade, fireworks, and lower price per night make it the easier sell for a party of four with a five-year-old.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a regular park ticket as well as a party ticket?

No, and buying both is the most expensive mistake in this season. A party ticket includes park entry from the published early admission time, several hours before the party itself begins. Treat the party night as your park day for that date and you save the full price of a day ticket, which was $119 to $209 in Florida and $104 to $214 in Anaheim as of July 2026.

Which sells out first, Oogie Boogie Bash or Not-So-Scary?

Oogie Boogie Bash, and it is not close. Disney California Adventure runs roughly 25 to 30 party nights against Magic Kingdom's 30 to 40, in a park with far less capacity, for a resort with a much larger local passholder base. Late-October weekend nights have historically sold out within hours of general sale. Not-So-Scary's early-season midweek nights are usually available well into the season.

Can adults wear costumes?

Yes, on party nights only, and both resorts publish rules that change from year to year. Costumes are not permitted for guests aged 14 and over on a normal park day at either resort. Masks, weapons and anything that obscures the face are restricted even on party nights. Check the operator's current costume policy before you pack, because this is one of the details Disney adjusts most often.

Is it worth going to Disney at Halloween if we do not buy a party ticket?

At the Disneyland Resort, yes. Halloween Time's ride overlays and seasonal entertainment are included with a standard ticket and change enough of both parks to justify the dates on their own. At Walt Disney World, no. The daytime offering is decoration and menu items; without a party ticket you are paying peak-adjacent prices for a normal park day.

Are the 2026 Halloween dates on this page confirmed?

No. Nothing on this page is presented as an announced 2026 date. The windows are the pattern across the 2023, 2024 and 2025 seasons, and the prices are 2025-season figures labelled as such. When we can verify 2026 dates against the operator's own calendar, the confidence ribbon on this page changes and the dates appear. Until then we would rather say we do not know.

How far ahead should we book a Halloween trip?

Six to nine months for Oogie Boogie Bash, three to four months for Not-So-Scary, and no more than that for anything else. Party tickets generally go on sale in late spring for the autumn season. Hotel rates in Orlando are at their annual low in late August and early September, so booking a room early buys you less than booking the event early does.
